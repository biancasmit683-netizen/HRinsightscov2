The picture is lopsided: how people are led and treated is a real strength — but what they are paid and the facilities they live with drag the whole score down. The weakest spots are concentrated, not company-wide, which makes them fixable.

What's working

Strengths to protect

  • Management is the top theme at 81% favourable — managers communicate clearly (84%) and are approachable (81%).
  • People feel safe — 84% psychologically safe, 83% physically safe, 83% comfortable speaking up in meetings.
  • Loyalty is high — 87% see themselves still here in 12 months, well ahead of what pay sentiment alone would predict.
  • Guides are the advocacy engine — 80% favourable, eNPS +69, the most engaged large team.
Where to act

Company-wide drags

  • Pay is the #1 issue — only 28% think their pay is fair, the lowest-scoring statement in the survey.
  • Living facilities score poorly — uniforms 32%, recreation areas 35%, on-site accommodation 41%, laundry 46%.
  • Reward fairness is questioned — just 47% feel recognition and rewards are handed out equally.
  • Only two themes sit below 50% — Pay & Reward (43%) and Facilities (47%) — and they pull everything down.
Specific managers

Where leadership is the issue

  • Daniel Okafor (Lodge E, 16 staff) — the clearest concern: team engagement 44% and a "My Manager" score of just 53%, the lowest of any sizeable team, with eNPS −31.
  • Sarah Bennett (Lodge A, 16 staff) — softer signal: manager rating 61% and overall 57%, advocacy barely positive (+6). Worth a supportive check-in.
  • Learn from Grace Molefe (13 staff) — 78% engagement, 85% manager rating, eNPS +23: the strongest management practice among teams still in place.
  • Not a manager problem: Marcus Reed's Lodge D team rate him highly (86%) yet score negative overall — their issue is pay and facilities, so don't "fix" the manager.
  • Watch the Guides after a leadership change — the most engaged large team (80% favourable, eNPS +69) rated its manager 93%, and that manager has since left. Worth monitoring for retention risk in the next wave.
Specific sites & departments

Where the pain concentrates

  • Lodge E is the weakest site — 48% favourable, eNPS −26. Most of the Lodge E team (16 of 19) reports to Daniel Okafor, so site and manager are the same problem.
  • Admin is the only department in the red — 50% favourable, eNPS −24. Spread across sites, so tackle it as a function, not one team.
  • Lodge D reads negative (eNPS −14) despite a well-liked manager — a pay-and-facilities problem specific to that lodge.
  • Lodge C is the standout lodge — 80% favourable, eNPS +55. Worth understanding what it does differently.
Reward spend

Where money will land best

  • Two benefits dominate demand — Transport (35%) and Medical Insurance (34%): together two-thirds of staff.
  • Staff want cash, not kind — 83% prefer a food allowance over a prepared meal (16%) or a food parcel (1%).
  • Translation: money, mobility and health cover beat in-kind perks — spend where it is actually wanted.
Tenure & timing

Who to win back

  • The "messy middle" is lukewarm — the 1–3 year cohort (80 people, over half the workforce) sits at 65% favourable.
  • Engagement builds with time — the 3–5 year group is happiest (80%, eNPS +41).
  • First-year staff (63%) start below average — an onboarding and early-experience opportunity.
